360 Studio

What makes play around a 360° screen work? A semester of prototypes and playtests to find out.

Role
Producer
Team
6 students · 2 advisors
Timeline
Aug – Dec 2026
Focus
Location-based multiplayer

Problem

A new screen is not a reason to play.

The Ring is a cylindrical display no one can see all of at once. Our first game worked — but it felt like a flat game wrapped around a cylinder.

How might we design play that only works around a 360° screen, and that strangers can pick up in seconds?

My role

I design how the team learns.

As producer I run our planning, playtests and interviews, external communication and documentation — so every prototype turns into evidence the team can act on.

Process

Four prototypes, one question each.

Asteroids

Does the device change how people move?

Yes — players walked around the screen to follow their ships.

Smash Slime

Can we design for movement?

70% of playtesters enjoyed the combat and moved around the Ring on purpose.

Undercooked

What if the controller is shared?

A rotating Lazy Susan made every tool pass a negotiation.

RingRacer

Does a continuous 3D world work?

Players loved the seamless world, but steering in depth was too demanding.

Playtest Day

Twelve groups, one shared format.

I designed a 20-minute session — three games, one short interview, the same four questions for everyone — so very different guests produced findings we could compare.

What’s next

Reflection

Fun and clarity are different measurements.

Guests could love a game and still not know which character was theirs. Measuring them separately made our findings actionable.
