VidViz

Lecture recordings are long, linear and hard to review. VidViz is a visual learning space, powered by generative AI, that turns a long video into a structure you can explore.

Problem

A mismatch between lectures and learning

Finding one ten-second explanation can mean scrubbing through fifty minutes of video. Recorded lectures have limited search, no structure and no summaries. They ask for passive listening at a fixed pace, while effective learning is active, exploratory and adapted to the learner.

My work

Prototyping new ways into a video

  • Flexible video segmentation — an interface for building hierarchical visual maps of a lecture, so learners can jump between topics instead of scrubbing.
  • Interactive prototypes — spider diagrams, advanced keyword search and a note-taking system that connects notes to moments in the video.
  • Roadmap — next steps toward a full platform, with AI automating segmentation and personalizing content.

Iteration

Two renditions before the final interface

Version 1 — spider diagram

Topics as nodes connected by lines, with AI-generated connections shown separately. It made the structure visible, but the web of links grew hard to read for a full lecture.

Version 2 — grouped nodes and a double timeline

Nodes grouped by their relationships, paired with a timeline view and a graph view, so learners could move between “where am I in the lecture” and “how do these ideas connect.”

Final — layered learning

The video is segmented into a hierarchy of main topic, subtopics and detail. A language model generates summaries, explanations and examples for each segment, and learners can take notes and search by keyword without scrubbing.

Next steps

From prototype to evidence

We closed the quarter with an evaluation plan rather than claims: an experiment with 10–20 learners comparing objective results with and without VidViz, plus surveys and ratings for the subjective experience. Beyond that, the roadmap moves from prototype to a coded implementation with more accurate automatic segmentation.
