NOM

College students were giving up on restaurant apps buried in options. NOM reimagines Yelp for students, with swipe-based recommendations that make choosing fast, personal and a little fun.

My role
UX Designer
Team
UCSD Design Co studio team
Methods
Surveys, affinity mapping, competitive audit, usability testing
Recognition
UX Project Design Studio Certification

Problem

Too many options, not enough decision

Students care about different things — price, atmosphere, how photogenic a place is, how quick it is to decide. Surveys and early usability sessions showed a clear pattern: when people faced long lists, they got frustrated or abandoned the search altogether. The challenge was to simplify the decision without making it feel generic.

Research

Mapping the landscape

We gathered findings into an affinity diagram and audited competitors across reviews (Yelp, Google reviews, Tripadvisor, the Michelin Guide) and delivery apps (Uber Eats, DoorDash, Postmates), comparing how easy their filters and navigation were to use. From this we wrote problem statements and design goals focused on the college-student context.

Four kinds of student eaters

Our personas showed why one ranked list fails: the on-campus foodie without a car who loves trying new cuisines; the commuter who cooks and is picky on the rare night out; the indecisive student who reads every review and still can’t choose; and the social eater for whom the atmosphere and friends matter more than the food. Storyboards put them in real moments — a late drive home through an unfamiliar town, a sunny beach day on a budget, a new student overwhelmed by options while carrying groceries.

Design

Borrowing the swipe

I researched how social apps use swiping to keep people engaged, and designed a recommendation interface that works like a cross between a dating app and a restaurant guide: quick, visual and personal. Around it we built a gallery-style home page, a search page with map integration, student-centric reviews, local discounts and a curated set of nearby restaurants, cafés and food trucks.

Testing

Two rounds of usability testing

I designed prototypes and led two rounds of usability testing with college students, iterating between rounds to improve usability and engagement.

Testing showed that swiping alone wasn’t enough for everyone: some students wanted quick, budget-friendly picks, others cared most about how a place looked. In the next round I added aesthetic filters alongside price, giving people control over what “a good match” meant without bringing back the long list. The lesson I carried forward was how to turn scattered, sometimes conflicting feedback into a small number of concrete design decisions.
